TreeNash on tree graphical games

Kearns, Littman & Singh, “Graphical Models for Game Theory”, UAI 2001 (KLS below). Step through the downstream and upstream passes on small trees.

Setting (KLS §3). An n-player two-action graphical game (G, ℳ) is an undirected graph G plus one local game matrix Mi per player. Mi is indexed by the actions of the neighbourhood NG(i), and that neighbourhood includes i. With |NG(i)| ≤ k, each matrix has 2k entries instead of 2n. Actions are 0 and 1. A mixed strategy pi ∈ [0,1] is the probability of playing action 0.

TreeNash (KLS §4, Fig. 1). Pick a root. Each vertex V has one downstream neighbour W (its “child”, towards the root) and upstream “parents” U1..k. On the downstream pass, V sends W a binary table T(w,v). An entry is 1 iff some Nash equilibrium of the subgame upstream of V (inclusive) has V = v, given W = w. That holds iff some witness u has T(v,ui) = 1 for every parent and v is a best response to (u, w). On the upstream pass, the root picks a feasible value and each vertex tells its parents what to play, using a witness. By KLS Theorem 1, the tables and witnesses represent all Nash equilibria.
