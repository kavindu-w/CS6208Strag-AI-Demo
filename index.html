Graphical games: two interactive companions

Small, inspectable implementations of the two papers covered in a CS6208 Advanced Topics in AI group project presentation. Each demo runs entirely in the browser, labels every modelling choice as coming either from the paper or from us, and is backed by an automated test suite.

Demo 1 · TreeNash

M. Kearns, M. L. Littman, S. Singh. Graphical Models for Game Theory. UAI 2001, 253–260.

  • Step through the downstream (table) and upstream (witness) passes on tree games.
  • Exact pure-strategy TreeNash, cross-checked against brute force.
  • ApproximateTreeNash on a τ-grid with ε-best responses. The heat maps show tables like KLS Fig. 2. Grid size comes from KLS Thm 5 or Ortiz's refinement, and the exact regret of the output is shown.
  • Matching pennies shows the gap between pure and mixed equilibria.

Demo 2 · Influence games

M. T. Irfan, L. E. Ortiz. A Game-Theoretic Approach to Influence in Networks. AAAI 2011, 688–694.

  • Build linear influence games, or load IO Fig. 1 or random / preferential-attachment instances.
  • Enumerate PSNE by brute force or with IO's pruned backtracking.
  • Choose S by hand, run IO's greedy step by step, or compute all optimal sets.
  • Theorem 4 dynamics, the Proposition 5 potential, the Theorem 7 O(nd) tree tables, and the Theorem 8(3) #KNAPSACK star.
  • A small replication of the random-LIG experiment.

Verification

Run node tests/run-tests.mjs (Node ≥ 18, no dependencies). It checks that:

  • KLS: pure TreeNash enumerates exactly the brute-force PSNE set on Irfan's slide example and on 120 random tree/root combinations (the pure-strategy analogue of KLS Thm 1, as in Irfan's slides). Every ApproximateTreeNash output on an Ortiz grid is an ε-NE by exact regret (90 cases). The τ formulas evaluate as stated.
  • Irfan's slides: our tables and witness lists for the threshold game of complements match pp. 7–9 of the slide PDF (two slides per page), apart from one root entry we believe is a typo (explained on the Demo 1 page).
  • IO:
    • Fig. 1 has exactly the six PSNE drawn in Fig. 1(b). {1,2,3} leaves two consistent PSNE and {1,6} leaves one.
    • On the Fig. 2 hypergraph, greedy yields {1,6}.
    • Backtracking with IO's pruning matches brute force on 260 random and preferential-attachment LIGs.
    • Greedy and exact sets always pin down the target.
    • The Thm 7 tables agree with generic TreeNash on 300 random tree LIGs.
    • The Thm 8(3) star counts equal knapsack counts.
    • The Prop 5 potential is exact.
  • Our slides: the examples on slides 60, 61, 62 and 67 are reproduced.

Remarks on the sources

  • IO Thm 7 inequality. The AAAI 2011 proof sketch prints the condition for Tij(xi, xj) = 1 without the threshold bi. The best-response condition xifi ≥ 0 requires it. We use xi(xjwji + ΣPa₁ x̂kwki − bi) + ΣPa₂|wti| ≥ 0. The test suite shows that the printed form disagrees with exact TreeNash on most random instances with b ≠ 0, while ours always agrees.
  • KLS Thm 5 and Ortiz's refinement. Ortiz's note (Dec. 2002) proves that a τ-grid with τ ≤ (1+ε)1/k − 1 contains an ε-NE, with k as below; his proof implicitly uses payoffs in [0,1] (KLS themselves assume |payoff| ≤ 1), and k = |NG(i)| including i. Hence τ ≤ ε/(2k) is also sufficient for ε ≤ 1, since then (1+ε)1/k − 1 ≥ ε/(2k); the note prints this last inequality in the opposite direction. Irfan's CSCI 3210 slides quote τ ≤ ε/(4d), with d the maximum degree; for d ≥ 1 that is a stricter condition, so it is also sufficient.
  • KLS Thm 5 is conservative. It is a sufficient condition; KLS's own Fig. 2 was computed with τ = 0.01 and ε = 0.05, far coarser than the theorem requires.
  • Logarithm base in KLS. KLS do not state it. Their Lemma 2 proof uses log(k/2) = log k − 1, which implies base 2.
  • Figures not reproduced. KLS Fig. 2/3 use payoffs that the paper does not give, so our M/O tree has the same shape but our own payoffs. IO's US Senate study needs learned models that we do not have.

Code provenance

We found no official code release for either paper. We searched GitHub and the authors' pages in October 2026. Two community implementations of pure-strategy TreeNash exist: axeldavy/PyTreeNash (Apache-2.0, a class project) and kylePellerin/TreeNash (no licence stated; also contains LIG code). We did not use or verify their code. Everything here was written from the papers and is checked by our own tests.

Layout: js/core/ holds the algorithms (plain ES modules, no dependencies, importable from Node). js/ui/ and js/*-page.js hold the interface. js/experiment-worker.js runs the replication in a background thread.
